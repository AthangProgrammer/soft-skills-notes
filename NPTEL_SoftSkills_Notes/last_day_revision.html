Last-Day Revision Sheet

Essential definitions, models, distinctions and facts, one block per lecture.

Week 1

L1. Introduction to Soft Skills

  • Soft skills = umbrella term: personality traits, social graces, language facility, personal habits, friendliness, optimism.
  • Hard skills get the job; soft skills make you excel. Communication is only a part of soft skills.
  • Key behavioural skills: self-awareness, flexibility, persistence and perseverance.
  • Vivekananda: arise (self-aware), awake (self-confident), stop not (dynamic).

L2. Aspects of Soft Skills

  • Aspects: communication, attitude, adaptability, teamwork, conflict management, leadership, time/space, mannerism, cultural sensitivity.
  • LSRW: listening (60–75% of time), speaking, reading, writing.
  • Hats: white objective, red emotion, yellow positive, green ideas, blue control.
  • Hume: truth springs from argument amongst friends; teams are the primary performance unit (Bidlake).

L3. Effective Communication Skills

  • Process: sender → encode → channel → receiver → decode → feedback.
  • Shaw: biggest problem is the illusion that communication has been accomplished.
  • Qualities: clarity, completeness, conciseness, correctness, courtesy, candidness (7 Cs).
  • Grapevine: informal, rumour-prone; internal flow upward, downward, horizontal, diagonal.

L4. Classification of Communication

  • Forms: intrapersonal, interpersonal (dyadic), extra-personal, group, mass/media.
  • Oral = spontaneous, immediate feedback; written = organised, record, delayed feedback.
  • Libel (written) vs slander (spoken); avoid plagiarism and misquoting.
  • 6 lakh+ non-verbal cues; meaning lies with the sender.

L5. Personality Development

  • Personality ← persona (mask); an inner set of qualities, not looks.
  • Id = pleasure; ego = reality (executive); superego = perfection (moral guardian).
  • Adler: social interest, fictional finalism, superiority, creative self.
  • “The child is the father of the man.”

Week 2

L6. Positive Thinking

  • Positive thinking = positive mental attitude (Peale).
  • Berne: OK/OK = positive and co-operative; OK/not-OK = imposing; not-OK/not-OK = introvert; not-OK/OK = negative.
  • Johari (Luft and Ingham, 1955): open, blind (unknown to self), hidden (unknown to others), unknown (to both).
  • Believe in your abilities; concentrate on goals.

L7. Telephonic Communication Skills Part I

  • Features: clarity, courtesy, consideration, brevity.
  • Make call: own call, agenda, identify self, purpose, polite close.
  • Receive call: within three rings, identify self, positive tone, accurate message.
  • Avoid negatives (‘No’, ‘You have to’); use ‘What can I do for you?’

L8. Telephonic Communication Skills Part II

  • Voice mail: substitute for memos/calls; solves time zones; no immediate feedback.
  • Teleconferencing: short notice; poor for negotiations; needs facilitator.
  • Video conferencing: three types; control body language; look at camera.
  • Cell etiquette: low tone, silent in public, no odd-hour calls.

L9. Communicating Without Words

  • Birdwhistell 35/65; Mehrabian 7/38/55.
  • Types: kinesics, proxemics, chronemics, paralanguage, haptics, meta-communication.
  • Kinesics: appearance, face, eyes, posture, movement, gesture.
  • Eyes: rapport and credibility; culture-specific.
  • Crossed arms/legs negative; open hands welcome.

L10. Paralanguage

  • Features: quality, volume, rate, pitch, pronunciation, pauses.
  • Rate 140–150 wpm; middle path.
  • Faults: throaty, breathy, nasal, harsh, muffled, hoarse.
  • Content words stressed; function words not.
  • Twain: rightly timed pause; Pythagoras: silence is better than unmeaning words.

Week 3

L11. Proxemics

  • Zones: 18 in / 4 ft / 12 ft / 30 ft+.
  • Intimate: family; personal: friends; social: strangers; public: speakers.
  • Low context: US, Germany, England; high context: Japan, China, Arab.
  • Time talks and space speaks (Hall).

L12. Haptics: The Language of Touch

  • Haptics = touch (Greek ‘I touch’).
  • Handshakes: dominant, submissive, equal, dead fish, glove.
  • Thailand: no head touching; Spain: 7 strokes; NZ: hongi.
  • Mulk Raj Anand: Untouchable (Bakha).
  • When unsure, restrict touch.

L13. Meta-communication

  • Meta = beyond.
  • Examples: ‘Leave him, not kill him’.
  • Silence: neutrality, hiding, disagreement, thinking time.
  • Non-words (ah, um) show doubt.
  • Rich: lying with silence; Kennedy pause.

L14. Listening Skills

  • Hearing = physiological; listening = mental.
  • Process: hear, filter, interpret, respond, remember.
  • Nathan: perceptive listening, sincere desire.
  • Barriers: prejudging, ambiguity, bias, rigidity.
  • 60–75% of time in listening.

L15. Types of Listening

  • Combative, attentive, reflective (most important).
  • Content, empathetic, appreciative, analytical.
  • External: noise, venue, time, culture.
  • Internal: ego, state of mind, language.
  • Take notes; do not interrupt; open mind.

Week 4

L16. Negotiation Skills I

  • Negotiation = argument + persuasion → agreement.
  • Formal: agenda, date, venue; informal: casual.
  • Place, time, atmosphere, fear, influence.
  • BATNA best fallback; WATNA worst; ZOPA overlap.
  • Aim at win-win.

L17. Negotiation Skills II

  • Phases: preparation, interaction, implementation.
  • Red, blue, purple (best).
  • You-attitude; sunny side.
  • Collaborate, accommodate, compromise, control, avoid.
  • Get what you negotiate.

L18. Culture as Communication

  • Culture = learnt traits, values, behaviour; dynamic.
  • Dimensions: context, individualism, formality, style, time.
  • US direct; Arabs and Asians relationship first.
  • GATT, NAFTA; global village.
  • Show deference, not difference.

L19. Communicating across Cultures

  • Culture = code learnt and shared.
  • Low context: words; high context: silence and relation.
  • Avoid ethnocentrism and stereotyping.
  • Six stages of ethno-relativism.
  • Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam.

L20. Organizational Communication

  • Scott: transmission + feedback → action.
  • Functions: forecast, organise, instruct, coordinate, control.
  • Roles: interpersonal, informational, decisional.
  • McGregor: control vs persuasion.
  • Use informal channels to supplement formal ones.

Week 5

L21. Communication Breakdown Part I

  • Breakdown at source, between people, in organisation.
  • Source: background, inference, rigid, frozen, allness.
  • People: perception, vocabulary, emotion, listening, selectivity, noise, semantic gap.
  • Twain’s lightning bug.

L22. Communication Breakdown Part II

  • Barriers: hierarchy, fear, attitude, transfer stations, media, jargon, noise, ethics.
  • Remedies: empathy, listening, open climate, fewer levels, feedback.
  • Message sent ≠ message received.

L23. Advanced Writing Skills

  • Bacon: writing maketh an exact man.
  • Conceptualise → research → draft → revise.
  • Direct plan: good news; indirect: bad news.
  • Nine patterns.
  • Short, simple, reader-centred sentences.

L24. Principles of Business Writing

  • 5 Cs.
  • Express, not impress.
  • many, soon, fully, I assume.
  • Negative in the middle; positive ending.
  • Eliot: no big words.

L25. Types of Business Writing Part I

  • Memo: informal, no salutation, designation in From, cc.
  • Letter report: 2–3 pages, inside address, close, enclosures.
  • Circular: awareness; four types.
  • Revise before sending.

Week 6

L26. Types of Business Writing Part II

  • Notice → agenda → minutes.
  • First/last agenda items.
  • Minutes past tense, signed.
  • No capitals in e-mail.
  • Research paper steps.

L27. Business Letters

  • 10 parts.
  • Dear Sir/Madam; Yours faithfully/sincerely.
  • Body in 3 paragraphs.
  • Enclosures with pages.
  • Neutral, direct tone.

L28. Business Letters: Format & Style

  • Full block, modified block, semi-block, simplified.
  • Full block: all left.
  • Simplified: no salutation/close.
  • You-approach; no blame.
  • Follow organisation's practice.

L29. Types of Business Letters Part I

  • AIDA.
  • Analysis, anticipation, adaptation.
  • Good news first; bad news softened.
  • Order: specific, payment.
  • Refusal retains goodwill.

L30. Types of Business Letters Part II

  • Collection: gentle → strong.
  • Claim: problem first; no blame; documents.
  • Adjustment: good news first; active; empathy; thanks.
  • Recommendation: honest, specific.

Week 7

L31. Report Writing

  • Reportare = re + portare.
  • Formal, factual, systematic, purpose- and audience-specific.
  • Objectives: update, evaluate, guide, inform, recommend, awareness.
  • Report vs literary table.

L32. Types of Reports

  • Five bases of classification.
  • Recommend only if asked.
  • Informative vs analytical.
  • Routine: fixed interval, proforma.
  • Special: critical, no format.
  • Bathing-suit quote.

L33. Strategies for Report Writing Part I

  • Purpose, audience, method, organise, outline.
  • TOR.
  • Primary vs secondary.
  • Observation cannot interpret.
  • Phone: reach vs depth.

L34. Strategies for Report Writing Part II

  • Interview: cues, control, costly.
  • Questionnaire: cheap, anonymous, low response.
  • Closed, rating, open, MCQ.
  • Tricky in the middle; covering letter.
  • Ruskin quote.

L35. Evaluation and Organization of Data

  • Probability: random, systematic, stratified.
  • Non-probability: convenience, judgment.
  • Decimal numbering, max 3 levels.
  • Coordination, subordination, parallelism.
  • Outline = half the job.

Week 8

L36. Structure of Reports: Part-I

  • Front matter, body, back matter.
  • Cover → abstract order.
  • Preface (author) vs foreword (expert).
  • Contents has pages.
  • Abstract < summary.

L37. Structure of Reports: Part-II

  • Introduction, discussion, conclusion, recommendation.
  • Conclusion: no new information.
  • Recommend if asked; feasible.
  • References cited; bibliography consulted.
  • APA vs MLA.

L38. Report Style: Part I

  • Simple, specific, familiar.
  • Avoid cliché, jargon, redundancy.
  • One modifier; no phrasal verbs.
  • Explain abbreviations.
  • Hawthorne.

L39. Report Style: Part II

  • Active vs passive.
  • Subject–verb close; emphatic order.
  • Split breathless sentences.
  • Unity, coherence, order.
  • Fog < 12; Orwell’s windowpane.

L40. Group Communication

  • Group vs team.
  • Formal, informal, virtual.
  • Groupthink; hidden agenda.
  • Cooperation, loyalty, cohesiveness.
  • Forming–Adjourning.

Week 9

L41. Leadership Skills

  • Autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire.
  • Transactional vs transformational.
  • Structure vs consideration.
  • Fiedler: fixed style.
  • Trust: integrity, competence, consistency, loyalty, openness.

L42. Group Discussion: Part I

  • GD unstructured, cooperative.
  • Debate structured.
  • 7–10 members; 15–25 min.
  • Topic vs case.
  • Driver, amiable, democrat, bulldozer.

L43. Group Discussion: Part II

  • Emergent leader.
  • Silent member = liability.
  • Listen, organise, speak early.
  • No chairman role.
  • Influence not dominate.

L44. Meeting Management

  • Decision is the main function.
  • Why, who, when, where, what.
  • Start on time; follow agenda.
  • Minutes official.
  • Deadlines and follow-up.

L45. Adaptability and Work Ethics

  • Adaptability = learning.
  • Ethics = right and wrong.
  • Kant: ends not means.
  • Seven rights.
  • Avoid plagiarism, pilfering, misquoting.

Week 10

L46. Advanced Speaking Skills

  • LSRW – speaking.
  • Immediate, emotive, spontaneous.
  • Voice, vocabulary, audience, style.
  • Familiar words.
  • Franklin quote.

L47. Oral Presentations, Speeches, and Debates

  • Inform, persuade, entertain.
  • Presentation: small group, aids.
  • Speech: occasion, no aids.
  • Debate: argument, rebuttal.
  • Courtesy.

L48. Combating Nervousness

  • Natural and universal.
  • Energising.
  • Prepare; know audience.
  • Pause, breathe, slow.
  • Record yourself.

L49. Patterns and Methods of Presentation

  • 5 Ps.
  • Chronological, causal, problem–solution, spatial, topical.
  • Manuscript, memorised, impromptu, extemporaneous.
  • Be brief in impromptu.
  • Outline for extemporaneous.

L50. Oral Presentation: Planning & Preparation

  • Purpose, topic, audience.
  • Familiar topic + new information.
  • Short spoken sentences.
  • Intro short; body 2–3 points.
  • Conclusion restates.

Week 11

L51. Making Effective Presentations

  • Hook start; emphatic end.
  • Practise, time, record.
  • Moderate rate; eye contact.
  • Slides = outline.
  • Connectives.

L52. Speeches for Various Occasions

  • Six types.
  • No visual aids.
  • Opening and closing key.
  • Tone matches occasion.
  • Brief, humble, warm.

L53. Interviews

  • Systematic oral process.
  • 9 types.
  • Confidence + information.
  • 7 stages.
  • Prepare and start early.

L54. Planning and Preparation: Part-1

  • Analyse yourself, identify skills.
  • Research organisation and job.
  • USP.
  • Intuitive vs learned skills.
  • Interview file; will to win.

L55. Planning and Preparation: Part II

  • Résumé: French; CV: Latin.
  • Purpose: interview call.
  • Chronological, functional, hybrid.
  • 1–1.5 pages.
  • Action verbs; no salary.

Week 12

L56. Drafting an Effective Resume

  • Résumé = passport.
  • Objective: 1–3 lines.
  • Education: latest first.
  • Cover letter: 3 paragraphs.
  • Show, don’t tell.

L57. Facing Job Interviews: Part- I

  • Prepare; be patient.
  • Let the interviewer lead.
  • Personality counts.
  • Navy/grey suit; black shoes.
  • Clear voice; use pauses.

L58. Facing Job Interviews: Part II

  • 7 question types.
  • Open at the start.
  • Affirmative and ethical answers.
  • No salary talk.
  • Sit when asked; close door gently.

L59. Emotional Intelligence and Critical Thinking

  • EI = recognise + manage emotions.
  • IQ gets job, EQ success.
  • 5 components.
  • Neutrality; non-judgmental.
  • Different, not indifferent.

L60. Applied Grammar

  • Police/clothes plural; news/politics singular.
  • No apostrophe in yours.
  • Nearest-subject rule.
  • A/an by sound.
  • Senior to; since vs for.
