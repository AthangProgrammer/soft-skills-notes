Practice Questions

Original practice questions with answers and explanations, by week and question type.

Week 1: Foundations of Soft Skills and Communication

MCQ

Q1 MCQ [L1] Which statement best describes soft skills?
  1. Technical expertise gained in college
  2. An umbrella term for people, life and behavioural skills
  3. Only communication skills
  4. Skills taught in every university syllabus
Answer: B
Why: The lecture calls soft skills an umbrella term and cautions that they are not merely communication skills.
Q2 MCQ [L1] Which of these is a hard skill?
  1. Persistence
  2. Friendliness
  3. Technical expertise in a subject
  4. Optimism
Answer: C
Why: Hard skills are subject knowledge and technical expertise.
Q3 MCQ [L2] Which hat is associated with generating ideas?
  1. White
  2. Red
  3. Green
  4. Blue
Answer: C
Why: Green hat = idea generation.
Q4 MCQ [L2] The word ‘communication’ comes from communicare, meaning:
  1. To speak
  2. To share
  3. To write
  4. To command
Answer: B
Why: Communication is sharing ideas or experiences.
Q5 MCQ [L3] The receiver’s task of extracting meaning from a message is called:
  1. Encoding
  2. Decoding
  3. Feedback
  4. Channelling
Answer: B
Why: Receivers decode; senders encode.
Q6 MCQ [L3] Shared background of experience between sender and receiver is the:
  1. Common frame of reference
  2. Grapevine
  3. Channel
  4. Feedback loop
Answer: A
Why: It makes communication successful and effective.
Q7 MCQ [L4] Which form of communication is also called dyadic?
  1. Intrapersonal
  2. Interpersonal
  3. Mass
  4. Group
Answer: B
Why: It involves two parties.
Q8 MCQ [L4] A defamatory spoken remark is called:
  1. Libel
  2. Slander
  3. Plagiarism
  4. Soliloquy
Answer: B
Why: Written = libel; spoken = slander.
Q9 MCQ [L5] The id operates on the:
  1. Reality principle
  2. Pleasure principle
  3. Moral principle
  4. Social principle
Answer: B
Why: The ego follows reality; the superego follows perfection.
Q10 MCQ [L5] Who proposed ‘fictional finalism’?
  1. Freud
  2. Hume
  3. Adler
  4. Shakespeare
Answer: C
Why: Alfred Adler.

MSQ

Q1 MSQ [L1] Select the behavioural skills named in the lecture.
  1. Self-awareness
  2. Flexibility
  3. Typing speed
  4. Persistence and perseverance
Answer: A, B, D
Why: These are the personal charms listed; typing speed is not mentioned.
Q2 MSQ [L2] Select the four communication skills discussed.
  1. Listening
  2. Speaking
  3. Reading
  4. Writing
  5. Painting
Answer: A, B, C, D
Why: LSRW are the four skills.
Q3 MSQ [L3] Which are directions of internal communication?
  1. Upward
  2. Downward
  3. Horizontal
  4. Subterranean
Answer: A, B, C
Why: Diagonal is also mentioned; ‘subterranean’ is not.
Q4 MSQ [L4] Select the examples of mass communication.
  1. Newspaper
  2. Television programme
  3. A conversation between two friends
  4. Talking to oneself
Answer: A, B
Why: They reach large audiences through devices.
Q5 MSQ [L5] Select the correct statements about the superego.
  1. It is the moral guardian
  2. It strives for perfection rather than pleasure
  3. It supports only the id
  4. It opposes both id and ego
Answer: A, B, D
Why: It does not support the id.

T/F

Q1 T/F [L1] Communication skills and soft skills mean exactly the same thing.
Answer: False
Why: Communication is only a part of the wider set of soft skills.
Q2 T/F [L2] Hearing and listening require the same amount of conscious effort.
Answer: False
Why: Hearing is physical and needs no conscious effort; listening is mental and conscious.
Q3 T/F [L3] The grapevine always gives authentic information.
Answer: False
Why: It is informal and may be rumour-based and inadequate.
Q4 T/F [L4] In written communication, feedback is usually immediate.
Answer: False
Why: Feedback in writing is delayed.
Q5 T/F [L5] Personality is derived from the Latin word meaning ‘mind’.
Answer: False
Why: It comes from persona, meaning mask.

Fill

Q1 Fill [L1] Soft skills are performance skills, while hard skills are part of the ______.
Answer: college curriculum
Why: Soft skills are not taught in colleges the way subject knowledge is.
Q2 Fill [L2] Business writing is meant to express and not to ______.
Answer: impress
Why: Avoid pompous words; be clear.
Q3 Fill [L3] Communication is complete only when ______ is received.
Answer: feedback
Why: Feedback shows the process has been completed.
Q4 Fill [L4] Soliloquies and asides are examples of ______ communication.
Answer: intrapersonal
Why: The speaker talks to himself.
Q5 Fill [L5] The executive of the personality is the ______.
Answer: ego
Why: It is also the organised portion of the id.

Short

Q1 Short [L1] Why were the two doctors in the lecture’s example differently successful?
Answer: Their soft skills differed.
Why: One used simple language, took the case history and had friendly staff; the other confused patients with jargon.
Q2 Short [L2] Name the five negative Cs that harm attitude in organisations.
Answer: Criticizing, complaining, comparing, competing and contending.
Why: They replace concentration on one’s own work.
Q3 Short [L3] Name two factors that can obstruct a message.
Answer: Noise in the environment and an unsuitable medium or language.
Why: Perception differences also matter.
Q4 Short [L4] Give two advantages of oral over written communication.
Answer: Spontaneity and immediate feedback and clarification.
Why: Voice also conveys emotion.
Q5 Short [L5] State two differences between Freud and Adler.
Answer: Freud stresses instincts and the past; Adler stresses social interest and future expectations.
Why: Adler also introduced the creative self.

Week 2: Positivity, Telephone and Non-verbal Communication

MCQ

Q1 MCQ [L6] In the Johari Window, the area unknown to the person but known to others is the:
  1. Open area
  2. Blind area
  3. Hidden area
  4. Unknown area
Answer: B
Why: Others see it; the person does not.
Q2 MCQ [L6] ‘I’m OK, you’re not OK’ describes a person who is:
  1. Positive
  2. Negative
  3. Imposing
  4. Introvert
Answer: C
Why: Only he matters; others are disregarded.
Q3 MCQ [L7] Which is the first and foremost feature of telephonic communication?
  1. Brevity
  2. Clarity
  3. Humour
  4. Confrontation
Answer: B
Why: The lecture lists clarity first.
Q4 MCQ [L7] A call should ideally be answered within:
  1. One minute
  2. Ten rings
  3. Three rings
  4. Whenever convenient
Answer: C
Why: Respond immediately, not more than after three rings.
Q5 MCQ [L8] Teleconferencing is generally ineffective for:
  1. Saving travel costs
  2. Negotiations
  3. Arranging meetings at short notice
  4. Connecting dispersed people
Answer: B
Why: Facial reactions and non-verbal cues cannot be read.
Q6 MCQ [L8] Voice mail helps solve ______ difficulties.
  1. Spelling
  2. Time zone
  3. Language
  4. Dress code
Answer: B
Why: Messages can be left when the person cannot be reached.
Q7 MCQ [L9] According to Mehrabian, the vocal element accounts for:
  1. 7%
  2. 38%
  3. 55%
  4. 65%
Answer: B
Why: 7% verbal, 38% vocal, 55% non-verbal.
Q8 MCQ [L9] The study of space in communication is:
  1. Kinesics
  2. Haptics
  3. Proxemics
  4. Chronemics
Answer: C
Why: Derived from proximity.
Q9 MCQ [L10] Hoarseness results from:
  1. Lazy lips
  2. Inflammation of the larynx
  3. Too much nasal air
  4. Fast speech
Answer: B
Why: It is a medical problem needing a speech therapist.
Q10 MCQ [L10] The ideal speaking rate suggested is:
  1. 80–100 wpm
  2. 140–150 wpm
  3. 200–220 wpm
  4. Any speed
Answer: B
Why: This is the balanced rate.

MSQ

Q1 MSQ [L6] Which are ways to develop positivism?
  1. Believe in your abilities
  2. Concentrate on your goals
  3. Imitate others completely
  4. Hide your weaknesses permanently
Answer: A, B
Why: The lecture warns against losing your own identity.
Q2 MSQ [L7] Select the guidelines for sending a call.
  1. Place your own call
  2. Plan an agenda
  3. Avoid identifying yourself
  4. Recline in your chair
Answer: A, B
Why: Always identify yourself and prepare an agenda.
Q3 MSQ [L8] Select correct statements about voice mail.
  1. It substitutes short memos and calls
  2. It solves time-zone problems
  3. It increases paperwork
  4. It gives immediate specific feedback
Answer: A, B
Why: It reduces paperwork and lacks immediate feedback.
Q4 MSQ [L9] Which are forms of kinesics?
  1. Eye contact
  2. Posture
  3. Touch
  4. Gesture
Answer: A, B, D
Why: Touch belongs to haptics.
Q5 MSQ [L10] Which words usually do NOT take stress?
  1. Articles
  2. Prepositions
  3. Nouns
  4. Conjunctions
Answer: A, B, D
Why: Nouns, adjectives, verbs and adverbs take stress.

T/F

Q1 T/F [L6] The Johari Window was proposed by Eric Berne.
Answer: False
Why: It was proposed by Joseph Luft and Harry Ingham in 1955; Berne gave the OK attitudes.
Q2 T/F [L7] In an official call it is good to begin with small talk about the weather.
Answer: False
Why: Small talk wastes money and time; be professional.
Q3 T/F [L8] In a video conference you should look at the camera and avoid parallel whispers.
Answer: True
Why: These are listed cautions.
Q4 T/F [L9] Non-verbal cues can completely substitute for words.
Answer: False
Why: They supplement, not substitute.
Q5 T/F [L10] Changes in pitch while speaking are called inflections.
Answer: True
Why: They convey feelings.

Fill

Q1 Fill [L6] Reducing the ______ area through self-disclosure builds trust.
Answer: hidden
Why: It is known to oneself but concealed from others.
Q2 Fill [L7] While transferring a call, ask the caller’s ______ and explain why you are transferring.
Answer: consent
Why: For example, “May I put you on hold?”
Q3 Fill [L8] On a cell phone one should speak in a ______ modulated voice.
Answer: low
Why: Loud calls disturb others.
Q4 Fill [L9] Clenched hands indicate ______ and frustration.
Answer: tension
Why: A fist signals determination.
Q5 Fill [L10] The ‘middle path’ in speech rate is called ______.
Answer: madhyam marga
Why: Neither too fast nor too slow.

Short

Q1 Short [L6] Why is ‘I’m OK, you’re OK’ the ideal attitude at work?
Answer: It is positive and co-operative, so it supports teamwork across different backgrounds.
Why: Teams need co-operation.
Q2 Short [L7] How should one respond to an angry customer on the phone?
Answer: Stay calm, listen, note the concern, assure a response and allow the caller to hang up first.
Why: This gives the caller more room.
Q3 Short [L8] What should a voice mail message include?
Answer: Name, point of the message, phone number and time/date of availability.
Why: Keep it focused on the essentials.
Q4 Short [L9] Why is eye contact culture-specific?
Answer: Some cultures gaze at the face; others, e.g. Asian, prefer peripheral gaze and treat direct gaze as impolite.
Why: Avoid both gaze and stare.
Q5 Short [L10] Why should a speaker use pauses?
Answer: They separate units of thought, give emphasis, let listeners absorb the message and help the speaker think.
Why: Mark Twain valued a rightly timed pause.

Short

Q1 Short [L11] How does office space show status?
Answer: Bosses have larger, corner rooms; lower employees have smaller cabins.
Why: Space denotes power and control.
Q2 Short [L12] What should a person do when unsure of another culture’s touch norms?
Answer: Restrict touch behaviour and respect local customs.
Why: Good intentions can be misread.
Q3 Short [L13] State two advantages of silence for a speaker.
Answer: It gives time to think and ignites new thoughts; it relieves monotony and wins attention.
Why: It also helps coherence.
Q4 Short [L14] Give two advantages of listening.
Answer: It is a means of gaining knowledge and it builds sound relationships; also improves vocabulary and performance.
Why: Listening saves time and money compared with reading alone.
Q5 Short [L15] Suggest two tips for effective listening.
Answer: Concentrate on the speaker with an open mind and take notes without interrupting.
Why: Avoid prejudging and parallel talk.

Short

Q1 Short [L46] How can voice be improved?
Answer: Voice quality is a gift but one can change the way one speaks through consistent training, clarity, modulation and practice, as singers do.
Why: Aim for clarity and variety.
Q2 Short [L47] Why prepare for the question–answer session?
Answer: Because the audience must be convinced before a decision; anticipating questions and answers helps persuasion.
Why: Q&A decides acceptance.
Q3 Short [L48] What should you do if you forget a point?
Answer: Review what you have said; repeating helps the audience and the next thought comes back.
Why: Nothing disastrous will happen.
Q4 Short [L49] Why is the memorised method risky?
Answer: Forgetting one phrase can halt the talk; pauses and transitions are hard to memorise; speed is hard to control and delivery sounds robotic.
Why: Not advisable for novices.
Q5 Short [L50] What should the conclusion contain?
Answer: An inference from the body, a restatement or recapitulation of main points, a call for action if relevant, and a natural signal that the talk is ending.
Why: Avoid abrupt endings.

Short

Q1 Short [L56] What is parallelism in a résumé?
Answer: Starting list items with the same grammatical form, e.g. past-tense action verbs such as supervised, coordinated, assisted.
Why: Makes bullets consistent.
Q2 Short [L57] State the jewellery advice for women at interviews.
Answer: Keep jewellery minimal: one set of earrings and no more than one ring on each hand.
Why: Avoid distraction.
Q3 Short [L58] How should you answer a question about your weakness?
Answer: Present it so that it becomes a merit, e.g. being a perfectionist who works hard to bring perfection to the task.
Why: Stay affirmative.
Q4 Short [L59] Differentiate IQ and EQ.
Answer: IQ measures mental or academic ability and helps in getting a job; EQ measures awareness and management of emotions in oneself and others and helps one succeed and lead.
Why: Balance both.
Q5 Short [L60] Differentiate ‘since’ and ‘for’.
Answer: ‘Since’ marks a specific point of time (since 2007); ‘for’ marks a period of time (for four years).
Why: Used with perfect continuous tenses.
